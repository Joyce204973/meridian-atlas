After a long day, you slide under the covers and close your eyes. Your body is heavy. Your mind is awake. You’re not stressed but your mind just won’t settle. You toss and turn and think about how to fall asleep. Or, you fall asleep fine, but your night is plagued by brief moments of wakefulness tossing and turning. Why do some people struggle with insomnia? Modern medicine and Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) both have a lot to say about the restless night. Let's read both maps.

## The TCM Map: Sleep is a Handover from Yang to Yin

TCM starts with Yin and Yang, two opposite but linked qualities: Yang is active, warm, and outward; Yin is quiet, cool, and inward. Wei Qi (卫气, "defensive Qi") is the protective energy that circulates near the surface of the body. By day it travels through the Yang, and at night it sinks into the Yin (Chen, 2011). Sleep, in this view, is a handover from Yang to Yin.

The classics describes this transition in the eyes. The Yin and Yang Qiao Mai (阴跷脉 and 阳跷脉, the "Motility vessels") meet at the inner corner of each eye. A passage in the Ling Shu, a classic text says that when Yang is abundant the eyes stay wide open, and when Yin is abundant they close (Deadman et al., 2007, p. 345).

In TCM, the Heart (心) is more than a pump. It is the home of the Shen (神), meaning "spirit" or "mind": your awareness, your emotions, your sense of being someone. A home needs walls, and the walls are made of Blood (血, Xue) and Yin. When they are weak, the Shen has nowhere to settle. The Spirit will "wander and roam," and what that feels like at 2 a.m.: restless, distracted, awake (Beinfield and Korngold, 1991).

## Why the mind doesn’t settle at night

Deadman's textbook sorts insomnia into five patterns, each pairing the Heart with another organ (Deadman et al., 2007, p. 191). I find it easiest to remember them as three kinds of unsettled night.

The first is a house with thin walls and a bare pantry. First is the Heart-Spleen pattern. Overthinking and worry wear down the Spleen (脾), the organ that makes Qi and Blood from food, so less Blood reaches the Heart. In the Heart-Gallbladder pattern, a sense of deep fear unsettles a Heart that is already weak. One would be timid, startled, and aware of the feeling of the heart beating.

The second is a house that overheats. The Kidney (肾) stores Yin and is supposed to send cooling Water upward to the Heart. When Kidney Yin runs low, the Heart's Fire burns unchecked, which is the Heart-Kidney pattern. In the Heart-Liver pattern, frustration and anger build Liver Fire that flares up the same way. Deadman suggests soaking the feet in hot water before bed to draw the excess Yang downward (p. 337).

The third is a house with too much going on downstairs. In the Heart-Stomach pattern, a heavy late meal or lingering phlegm-heat in the Stomach disturbs the Shen from below. Phlegm heat is caused by consuming more spicy, oily, baked foods and sweets than your body can process which causes internal circulation to be like a thick like a swamp.

In all three, TCM asks the same question: what is keeping the Shen from settling? Insomnia becomes a symptom of many possible imbalances, shaped by emotion, stress, diet, and lifestyle, all interplaying.

## What is sleep in biomedicine?

Biomedicine looks at the same night and asks which switches are flipped on when they’re supposed to be off. The first is sleep pressure. The longer you are awake, the more adenosine builds up and binds to adenosine receptors, which makes your brain sleepy. The second is the circadian clock, which is run by a small region of the brain called the suprachiasmatic nucleus. The 24-hour cycle is run by sunlight and sets the timing for alertness, cortisol, and melatonin, which is the hormone the body sends out at night. Sleep pressure tells your brain how much sleep it needs, and the clock tells it when. In insomnia, the body struggles to follow these two systems because something keeps the body awake.

The leading explanation of insomnia in biomedicine is hyperarousal: a nervous system stuck on. The evidence shows that people with insomnia tend to show moderately higher levels of cortisol, the stress hormone, and faster brain-wave activity during sleep, (Dressle & Riemann, 2023).

In the cognitive model of insomnia, worry about not sleeping raises arousal, which makes you monitor yourself (checking the clock, scanning your body), which makes you overestimate how bad your sleep is, which fuels more worry (Harvey, 2002). TCM has a similar line: overthinking weakens the Spleen’s energy, Blood runs low, the Heart's house empties. Both systems of mapping identify overthinking as the fuel.

## Western Treatment for Insomnia

Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the first-line treatment doctors prescribe. It includes sleep education, cognitive work on worry, relaxation, stimulus control (leaving the bed if you're awake after about 15 to 20 minutes), and sleep restriction (briefly limiting time in bed to your actual sleep time so sleep pressure builds). Studies show that for every 2 people treated with CBT-I, at least 1 person would improve, and in one trial a 6-week course kept its benefit for at least 24 months (Sutton, 2021).

Because biomedicine maps sleep and wake periods down to the molecular level, treatment solutions also include drugs that target individual steps of the pathway. As we discussed above, wakefulness is defined by arousal circuits firing through the brain, which release orexin, histamine, and noradrenaline, signaling molecules that keep the system awake. Sleeping pills such as benzodiazepines and newer "Z-drugs" work to amplify the effect of GABA. The more GABA the neuron has, the less likely it is to fire. Newer orexin antagonists such as suvorexant block orexin, the signal that holds the wake system on. However, results are modest, and the placebo effect explains up to 60% of the response (Sutton, 2021).

## Needling by pattern

Unlike sleep drugs which are universally prescribed for those with insomnia, acupuncture treatment is more uniquely tailored to the individual.. They first work out which pattern is behind the insomnia, then choose points for that pattern. Two points are very common. Shenmen HE-7 ("Spirit Gate," on the pinky-side wrist crease) is the Heart's source point. It is used in any pattern to restore peace to the Shen. Sanyinjiao SP-6, on the inner shin above the ankle, is where the three leg Yin channels (Spleen, Liver, Kidney) meet, so it reaches the Blood and Yin that make up the Shen's house (Deadman et al., 2007).

For the thin-walled Heart-Spleen night, Zusanli ST-36 supports the Spleen-Stomach, the source of Qi and Blood, and Yintang, between the eyebrows, calms the mind. For the overheated Heart-Kidney night, Taixi KID-3 nourishes Kidney Yin, and Zhaohai KID-6 is the point tied to the Yin Qiao vessel and the eyes. For Liver Fire, Anmian ("Peaceful Sleep," just behind the ear) joins the back-shu points BL-18 and BL-19, which reach the Liver and Gallbladder from the back. For Gallbladder fright, Danshu BL-19 is the key point. For the crowded Heart-Stomach night, Lidui ST-45 on the second toe is one of several toe points used to draw rising Fire downward (Deadman et al., 2007).

## How Effective is Acupuncture for Insomnia?

The clinical studies on whether acupuncture is effective for treating insomnia are "promising, but unproven." A review pooled 33 trials and judged all of them at high risk of bias, so we can’t say it supports or refutes acupuncture (Cheuk et al., 2012). A 2025 meta-analysis of 25 trials compared acupuncture with sedative-hypnotic drugs and found better sleep-quality scores for acupuncture, but results varied. Almost no assessors were blinded, and none followed patients for long, so results just aren’t clear (Ma et al., 2025). Another trial gave electroacupuncture to 270 people with depression and insomnia. The acupuncture group beat both sham needling and standard care over eight weeks (Yin et al., 2022). Not only are empirical studies on acupuncture lacking in the first place, but the methods are weak and invite lots of bias, and importantly, the Yin trial used the same seven points for everyone. This neglects the power that TCM has: it looks at each body as a unique system. A stronger clinical test would identify the pattern and treat with unique point combinations.

## What holding both maps shows

Both maps treat insomnia as the symptom of many deeply rooted problems, and both connect sleep to emotion. Both systems recommend less caffeine, less alcohol, no heavy late meals.

Each map treats the problem based on how they map the problem. The Western map splits the night into an overactive wake system and a learned loop of worry, so it targets both: drugs quiet the wake system, and CBT-I unwinds the loop, which is why guidelines put CBT-I first (Sutton, 2021). The TCM map reads the whole house, so it treats the pattern, with needles and often herbs, food, and lifestyle changes alongside them.
